Scope

What brand work covers.

Representative example.

  • Identity marks. A primary mark plus the variants a real business actually needs: horizontal, stacked, icon-only and single-colour.
  • Palette. A defined set with the contrast checked for accessibility, not a mood board.
  • Type. A working type system, sans-serif per the owner's stated preference, with a display role and a body role defined, chosen for how it behaves at the sizes your pages genuinely use.
  • Tone. How the business sounds in a heading, in a service page and in a follow-up email, written down so it can be applied by somebody who was not in the room.
  • Application. The identity carried across the site templates, the campaign assets, the ads and the profiles, so the same business appears everywhere the campaign puts it.

How it begins

Discovery, then direction, then application.

Representative example.

  1. Discovery. What the business actually is, and who it is competing against in the result list.
  2. Direction. A small number of routes, shown as real applications rather than as abstractions.
  3. Application. The rollout across the site and the campaign assets, and then the ongoing upkeep as new assets are produced each month.

The work is continuous rather than a project with a handover date, and it continues in a month billing is paused.

The agreement is assessed month by month rather than as one test at the end.

The mechanic

How this is assessed and billed.

  1. A monthly retainer is agreed up front.
  2. Each month is assessed against the ranking metrics named in the contract.
  3. In a month we are off target, the brand work continues and your billing stops.
  4. Billing resumes when results are back on track.

You stop paying for that month. The obligation is suspended rather than erased, and the work carries on through it.

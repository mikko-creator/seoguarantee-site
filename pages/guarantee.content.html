07 A month off target

Three things happen.

  1. It is automatic, and it was written in before you signed. The pause is a contract trigger, not a concession you have to ask for after a bad quarter.
  2. Nothing is handed back, because nothing was charged. The obligation for that month is suspended, not erased after the fact.

10 The seven answers

Is a guaranteed ranking a red flag? Seven answers, in the order the case against it is usually made.

If you have read the standard warning article about this category, you have read seven arguments.

01

Search engines warn against guaranteed rankings. Conceded.

Google Search Central states: "No one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google." Google's own beginner documentation goes further: "If they guarantee you that their changes will give you first place in search results, find someone else." Both are correct. Nothing we do gives us control of a ranking algorithm, Google does not approve, endorse or permit a promise of position, and we make no suggestion otherwise. What is under our control is our own invoice.

02

You cannot make a guarantee about something you cannot control. Correct.

Which is why what we guarantee is not the algorithm. It is the billing. We control when we bill, absolutely and unilaterally, and we have written into the contract that we do not bill in a month that fails the assessment in S2.

03

A guaranteed service may involve tactics that risk a penalty.

A fee tied to a position is a fee that rewards shortcuts. The answer is the published method in S5 and the standing commitment in S11: we do not use a tactic that puts your domain at risk of a search penalty. A penalty ends the positions, which ends the invoice, and the recovery work would then run at our cost for as long as it took.

04

Guaranteed rankings may provide little benefit.

The specific charge is that the guaranteed phrases are the easy ones. S5 is the answer: the phrases are proposed from competitive research and put on the schedule you sign, so you can judge whether those phrases would produce work for you.

05

One-size-fits-all strategies do not work.

Agreed. See the pages for dental practices, law firms, medical spas, HVAC businesses and contracting businesses. A dental practice and an HVAC business are not competing for the same kind of search at the same moment.

06

The wrong tactics can hurt your business.

Also true, which is why S11 exists and states what this guarantee does not cover.

07

Page-one rankings in 30 days.

The 6- and 12-month windows quoted and attributed in S6 sit inside the three-to-six-month band the category's own critics publish as realistic, and the agreement itself is assessed on the monthly cadence in S2.

And one more

One thing we will not claim.

This offer is a guaranteed SEO service by the standard definition: a service promising a specific result, typically first-page rankings for a set of key phrases, inside a timeframe. We are inside that category, not beside it.

We are not asking you to believe the promise. We are asking you to check whether we delivered, before you pay for it.

11 The boundary

What this guarantee does not cover.

Part one

What we will not do.

We do not use a tactic that puts your domain at risk of a search penalty. That is a standing commitment about our own conduct.

Part two

What the guarantee is not.

  • It is not a promise of a specific position on page one. First page means what S6 defines it to mean, and nothing narrower.
  • It is not a promise of a sales figure, a revenue number or a lead volume. We make no claim about what your business will earn.
  • It is not money returned to you. Billing pauses and later resumes, and the obligation is suspended rather than erased.
  • It is not a promise about paid placement, map results or AI-generated answers, unless your contract names them.

What counts as back on track?

The month clears the same assessment the pause was triggered by, read from the same monthly ranking report. It is the same test in both directions.

Who verifies the ranking, and in what tool?

This is a contract term. Ask on the call, and hold us to the answer in writing.

What happens if I change my site mid-campaign?

Tell us first. Site changes are part of the work and most of them are ours to make anyway, since web and development sit inside the same fee. A change made without us that moves a contracted position is the kind of thing the agreement has to account for.

Spend is not the metric. If a phrase becomes undeliverable for your business, the honest moment to say so is at the consultation, before it is on the schedule. If it becomes undeliverable later, the billing consequence is the one in S2: we are off target, so we keep working and you stop paying.

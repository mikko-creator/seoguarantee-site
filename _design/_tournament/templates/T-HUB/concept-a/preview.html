The wedge

Why the whole stack sits inside one fee.

What you are probably running now

An SEO retainer with one firm. A web agency that owns the site. A conversion consultant brought in when the numbers stall. A brand designer somewhere else. Four contracts, four invoices, four sets of priorities, four calendars, and not one of those agreements conditioned on delivering anything in particular.

What outcome pricing forces

The whole stack is included because we are paid on the outcome rather than on the hours. Holding the web work back to sell it separately would only make our own job harder and our own invoice less likely to go out. Bundling is not generosity.

The posture we are describing

The conventional model runs the other way.

It publishes monthly retainer rates and hourly consulting rates and attaches no delivery condition to either, so breadth is something to be sold in pieces. That is a posture, not a flaw in anyone's character, and we are describing it rather than pricing against it.

The honest limit, stated here rather than buried: this is a billing term. It is not a claim about controlling Google, and Google does not approve, endorse or permit a promise of position.

Owner ruling outstanding

What the agreed fee does NOT cover

This has never been stated by the business, anywhere. There is no exclusion list on the live site and none was supplied at intake. It is not invented here, because a guessed exclusion list is a contract dispute in month two. It ships as a visible gap until the owner rules it, and the answer belongs in the agreement before it belongs on this page.

One note on how this market usually opens the conversation. The common first step is a form asking you to select a monthly budget band before anyone has told you what will be delivered. Those bands are a record of what buyers are asked to declare about themselves, not a rate card, and none of them appears on this site as anyone's price. This page runs the sequence the other way round: what is delivered, how it is measured, and then a fee quoted per business after a competitive review.

The bounded position

Where we stand on AI search.

Three things, and then we stop.

01

What we do about it

AI Overviews and assistant answers are assembled from the same pages, the same structure and the same authority the ranking work is already building. So the work does not change: it is a consequence of what is already in your contract, not a separate product with its own line.

02

What the guarantee is written against

First-page organic positions on named key phrases in a named geography. AI surfaces are not part of the contracted metric, and we are not going to pretend otherwise in order to sound current.

03

What we do not claim to control

How an assistant chooses to summarise a result, whether it cites a source at all, and how any of that changes next quarter. Nobody controls it, and a number attached to it would be a number without a period, a baseline or a source.

That last point is why this section carries no figures. Where we have no method of our own to publish, restraint is the honest position rather than a gap in the copy.

The dental phrase ledger

What the consultation produces is a ledger, and what the contract names is the same ledger.

It has four columns: the key phrase, the search geography it is measured in, what the patient is doing at the moment they type it, and who holds page one for it today. That last column is what decides whether a phrase is worth contracting at all.

Geography, stated honestly: dental catchment is neighbourhood tight, and nobody drives across a metro for a cleaning. So the set is proposed per location, and a two-site practice is two phrase sets rather than one longer list.

Method: we read who currently holds page one for each candidate phrase, propose a list from that read, and then the list is signed. Signing it is what turns "delivered" into something checkable instead of something arguable.

The published case

Four charges, and where the edge of the work sits.

You have probably already read the case against guaranteed search work. It is well written, it quotes the search engines' own documentation, and a page that pretends otherwise loses to it. So here are its four charges and four answers.

CHARGE 01

"Search engines warn against ranking guarantees."

They do, and they are right to. Nobody can guarantee a specific ranking position and this offer does not claim to. Nothing here suggests that a search engine approves of, permits or endorses an arrangement like this one, because none of them does.

CHARGE 02

"You cannot guarantee what you do not control."

Also true, and it is exactly why the guarantee is not about the algorithm. What is guaranteed is a term of business: when the invoice is owed and when it is not. We carry the risk of having been wrong about a phrase instead of handing it to you.

Charge 03

"Guaranteed work may involve tactics that get a site penalised."

The answer to that one is publication rather than reassurance. Both lists below are the whole answer, and a dentist can check every line of either without an SEO vocabulary, which is the point of writing it down.

CHARGE 04

"One-size-fits-all does not work."

Correct, and the ledger above is the answer to it rather than a paragraph here. The full argument, with the terms in the language they are written in, lives on guarantee.

On timing: the current site publishes 6- and 12-month windows, which are not yet an agreed contract term, while the agreement is assessed month by month throughout. The industry's own published realism band is three to six months, and the pattern buyers are told to watch for is page one in 30 days. Stating a slower, longer window than the pattern is the strongest honesty signal this page has, so it is not softened.

Before the call

Five questions, answered where you can read them.

01

Is a guaranteed ranking a red flag?

Usually, yes, and a firm promising you a position by a date is a firm to check carefully. The difference here is what the promise is about. We are not telling you how a search engine will behave. We are telling you when an invoice is owed.

02

Who chooses the phrases?

We propose them from competitive research and they are named in the contract you sign. You do not get to strike one off the list afterwards, and nothing is swapped once signature has happened.

03

What actually happens in a month you are off-target?

The work carries on at full scope and the billing stops until the positions are back on track. It is automatic and written into the agreement before anyone signs, so it is not something you have to argue for after the fact.

04

How long does this take?

The site currently publishes 6- and 12-month windows, which are not yet an agreed contract term. The agreement is checked against the phrases every month throughout rather than settled by a single test at the end.

05

My competitor is a large dental group with a bigger budget.

Then the answer is the phrase set and the intent tiers, not the spend. The commercial point is simpler than scale: their agency is owed its retainer whether or not the agreed metrics land, and the risk reversal usually offered elsewhere is the liberty to cancel next month, which does nothing about the month that already missed.

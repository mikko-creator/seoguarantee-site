## §1 hero — S

H1 must state the bundle AND the payment condition in one breath. The promise never travels alone. Working line for the builder to sharpen, not to replace: "SEO, web, UI/UX, development, CRO, graphic design and branding under one agreed monthly fee — on the key phrases named in your contract, or you don't pay."

Subline states the mechanic in one sentence: the fee is agreed up front, each month is assessed against the agreed metrics, and any month we are off-target we keep working and your billing stops. Not a refund. Not money-back. Not risk-free.

Hero visual slot: describe it in concept-agnostic terms. The hero carries ONE motion slot whose job is to dramatise either the position being taken or the billing condition being applied. Do not hard-wire a single treatment into the copy or the layout — the animation concept is provisional and may be swapped at the build's FEEL gate, so the headline, subline and CTA must read correctly against any of the candidate treatments, and the poster frame alone must carry the page if motion is suppressed.

No price. No figure. No exclamation mark. No emoji. The live H1 on this exact page, "WEB + CRO + SEO - ONE PRICE" (with its live exclamation mark), is a banned string and does not survive in any form.

## §2 why-one-agreed-fee-wedge — M

The defensive core. State the status quo the reader is actually living in: an SEO retainer from one firm, a web agency for the site, a CRO consultant, a brand designer — four contracts, four invoices, four sets of priorities, and not one of them conditioned on delivering anything.

Then the load-bearing sentence, which must be written as a CONSEQUENCE and never as a breadth boast: the whole stack is included BECAUSE we are paid on the outcome rather than on the hours. If the fee is owed only while the agreed metrics are being delivered, then withholding the web work or the conversion work to sell it separately would only make our own job harder. Bundling is not generosity and must not be written as generosity — it is what outcome-pricing forces.

Contrast against the incumbent retainer posture: the conventional agency model publishes monthly retainer rates and hourly consulting rates and attaches no delivery condition to either. Write that as a posture, not as a number — do NOT reproduce any competitor dollar figure on the page. A reader does not parse whose number it is, and a figure on this page reads as our figure, which D-001 forbids site-wide.

Also name the honest limit: this is a billing term, not a claim about controlling Google. Never imply Google endorses, approves or permits ranking guarantees.

Required data: D-POSITION-W3, D-INCUMBENT-001-PRICING

## §3 what-is-included — the seven service blocks — M

Exactly SEVEN blocks, one per routed leaf, each one linking to its deep page. This is the T-HUB floor: one sub-area block per routed leaf, every block links out.

1. SEO Services -> seo-services. First page on the key phrases named in your contract, or you don't pay. The other six exist to protect and convert this one.
2. Web Design -> web-design. The site itself, built to hold the positions. Absorbs the equity of the retired /services/las-vegas-web-design/ page, so this block must carry real substance, not a one-liner.
3. UI/UX Design -> ui-ux-design. Turning won traffic into enquiries. Net-new page with no live predecessor: the block must be written as clearly-swappable representative direction and flagged for owner copy.
4. Web Development -> web-development. Fast, crawlable, indexable builds. Also net-new with no live predecessor, same flag.
5. CRO Services -> cro-services. Converting the rankings we are contracted to deliver. Absorbs /services/las-vegas-conversion-rate-optimization/, /cro-page/ and two off-sitemap duplicates, so it likewise carries real substance.
6. Graphic Design -> graphic-design. Ads, social and site assets, inside the same fee.
7. Branding -> branding. Identity work, inside the same fee.

Every block ends on the same clause, in the same words each time: included in the one agreed monthly fee. That repetition is the point — it is what stops the mega menu from reading as an ordinary agency service list and diluting the wedge.

The live suite page's own card set is the anchor for what is genuinely offered: Search Engine Optimization, Website Design and Development, Content Writing, Reputation Management, Conversion Optimization, Graphic Design, Branding, Consulting. Content Writing, Reputation Management and Consulting have no mega-menu leaf of their own — name them in a closing line of this section as included capabilities so the offer is not silently narrowed by the nav, but do NOT invent pages for them.

Banned on every block: "Best In Class Web Design Guarantee". There is exactly ONE guarantee and web design is included in it, never separately guaranteed. Also banned here: the live typo "desireable" and the sentence "we are without a doubt the company for you".

## §4 what-the-one-fee-replaces — M

Short, concrete, and framed as substitution rather than savings. What the one fee replaces: the separate web build quote, the separate conversion engagement, the separate brand and graphic work, the separate content writer. What it does NOT replace stays honest and is the section's hardest requirement.

The anti-product line — what is explicitly NOT included — is UNFILLED upstream. It is recorded as an open owner gap and must NOT be invented. Ship this block with a visible OWNER-PENDING placeholder naming exactly what is missing: the list of work the agreed fee does not cover. A brief that guesses this creates a contract dispute in month two.

Framing note for the builder: the competitor capture that sits behind this section is a lead-form budget-band selector — evidence that buyers in this market are asked to self-select a monthly spend before anyone has told them what will be delivered. Those bands are the prospect's self-declared budget, NOT a competitor rate card, and they may not be presented as anyone's prices. This page inverts the sequence: state what is delivered and how it is measured first, and the fee is quoted per business after a competitive review. No band, no figure, no ladder, no "starting at" appears on the page.

Required data: D-MARKET-001-PRICING, D-OFFER-018

## §5 ai-search-position — S

The bounded AI-search section. The architecture folds this in here deliberately rather than building a standalone page, because what is warranted is a stated POSITION, not a service line the client has not evidenced.

Say three things and stop. (a) What we actually do about AI Overviews and assistant answers, described as a consequence of the ranking and content work already in the contract, not as a separate product. (b) That the guarantee is written against first-page organic positions on named key phrases in a named geography — AI surfaces are not part of the contracted metric and we do not pretend otherwise. (c) Plainly, what we do not claim to control.

ZERO metrics in this section. Explicitly do not mirror the competitor's AI-visibility percentage style: those figures are self-reported with no stated methodology, and we have no methodology of our own to publish. A number without its period, baseline and source is not usable copy.

This section is also where the site's own banned-claim discipline is visible: the offer-level banned-claims field is unfilled upstream, which is precisely why the default here is restraint rather than a claim.

Required data: D-MARKET-001-AI, D-OFFER-023

## §6 proof-data-point — S

The single proof element, and it is deliberately thin. Exactly TWO permitted items:

(a) "Rated 4.9 from 35 Google reviews" — always stated WITH the count, never the rating alone. The modest count is the point; it is what makes the number credible next to competitors quoting four figures.

(b) One real capture of the client-portal monthly reporting view showing tracked key phrases and their positions. This is product UI, not a metric, and it is the honest way to show that "assessed month by month" is a thing that physically happens. It ships behind a visible OWNER-PENDING token until a real screenshot lands in the inbox. Under the fabricated-fallback proof strategy a representative placeholder may stand in so the build does not stall, but it must be visibly marked as representative and must never read as a real client's data.

NO percentage tiles on this page. Not one. The ~17 bare tiles across 10 named clients on the live site are unsubstantiated, and the aggregate strips built from them are worse. No press logo wall. No republished testimonial that asserts a number-one ranking.

Route to results for anything stronger — that page is where named, dated, method-stated outcomes live.

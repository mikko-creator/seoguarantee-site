## §3 capability-deep-dive — the treatment-phrase demand map — M
The section that makes this a real med spa page. Build it as a visible map with the columns [treatment phrase] · [search geography] · [what the searcher is deciding] · [who holds page one today] — and make the fourth column do the work, because it carries this vertical's single most useful and least obvious fact.

That fact: a med spa competing for a treatment phrase is usually not competing against other med spas. The page-one holders for a procedure name are commonly dermatology practices, plastic surgery groups, hospital-affiliated clinics and national chains with a page per location. The competitor set changes phrase by phrase, which is exactly why a single "med spa" strategy underperforms, and it is the honest answer to the charge that guaranteed SEO sells one-size-fits-all work.

Set out the demand in its two layers:
(a) Category phrases — "med spa [city]", "medical spa [city]", "medical spa near me". Fewer searches than people assume, and the searcher already knows the category.
(b) Treatment phrases — body contouring, laser treatments, injectables, skin resurfacing, hair removal, each with a city or a "near me". Collectively far larger, fragmented across many separate phrases, and where the buying decision is actually made. These are also the phrases where a spa's real service menu differs from the spa down the road, which makes them the honest place to compete.

Geography rule: aesthetic clients will travel further than a dental patient but not across a metro, and demand concentrates around specific neighbourhoods and around the events people book treatments before. State the catchment as something the consultation defines, not as a rule of thumb the page invents.

Verified live phrase strings usable as examples of the map's SHAPE only: "med spa sf", "medical spa san francisco", "body contouring", "laser treatments fort lauderdale". Every example is illustrative of the structure, never a position the agency currently holds.

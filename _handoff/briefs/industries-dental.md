## §3 capability-deep-dive — the dental phrase ledger — M
This is the page's spine and the section that makes it a real dental page rather than the vertical-swapped template the live site ships. Build it as a visible artifact, not a paragraph: a ledger with the columns [key phrase] · [search geography] · [what the patient is doing when they type it] · [who holds page one today]. That ledger is what the consultation produces and what the contract names.

Group the dental set into its three genuine intent tiers, because dental demand is not one behaviour and this is the substance a generic SEO page cannot fake:
(a) Emergency and same-day — "emergency dentist", "emergency dentistry", tooth pain plus a city. Unplanned, decided inside the hour, the least brand-loyal search in the trade, and almost always on a phone.
(b) Procedure-led — oral surgery, implants, root canal, each with a city. Planned, researched over days, materially higher case value, and the tier where a practice's actual specialisms differ from the practice next door.
(c) The contested generics — "dentist near me", "dentist [city]", "dental clinics in [city]". Highest volume, every practice in the metro chasing them, and the slowest tier to move.

State the geography rule honestly: dental catchment is neighbourhood-tight, patients do not drive across a metro for a cleaning, so the phrase set is proposed per location and a two-site practice is two phrase sets rather than one longer list.

Method of proposal, stated as method: competitive research against the practices currently holding page one for each candidate phrase, then a proposed list, then a signed list. The point of signing it is that "delivered" becomes checkable instead of arguable.

Verified live phrase strings usable as examples of the ledger's SHAPE only: "dentist near me", "emergency dentistry", "oral surgery Fort Lauderdale", "dental clinics in rancho cucamonga". Every example must read as illustrative, never as a position the agency currently holds.

## §2 persona-pillars — M
Exactly 3 pillars, one short paragraph each, written to a contractor who runs trucks and a dispatch board. Competitor displacement is the frame.

Pillar 1 — emergency demand is won or lost at the moment of search, and there is no second chance at it. A repair search is decided in minutes by someone who has never heard of any company on the page. There is no brand memory to fall back on, no comparison shopping, and no return visit next week. Either the phrase is answered by your listing or the job is somebody else's.

Pillar 2 — seasonal peaks make a missed month expensive in a way it is not in other trades. Demand in this business is not spread evenly across a year; it compresses into the first heat wave and the first hard freeze. A position that arrives in October is not worth what the same position is worth in June. Say this as a fact about the trade, not as a reason to hurry into a contract.

Pillar 3 — the phrases AND the service area are named in the contract before you sign. One line here; the method section carries the detail. The agency proposes the set from competitive research and the contractor signs it. No veto, no substitution right. The service-area boundary is part of what gets named, which is unusual and worth stating, because in this trade the geography is the campaign.

## S3 why-brand-work-belongs-inside-a-ranking-contract

<!-- src: D-POSITION-W3, D-OFFER-019, D-OFFER-013 -->

This is the load-bearing part of the page, because branding is the line nobody expects to be included and a buyer is right to ask what the catch is.

**1. It is not a gift.** It is inside a fee that is owed only while the contracted ranking metrics are being delivered. When they are not, we keep working on your brand and your billing pauses.

**2. We are paid on the outcome rather than the hours.** Once that is the model, we have a direct interest in the thing the traffic lands on being credible, because a position that produces nothing for you is a position we still have to go on delivering next month. Your brand looking unconvincing is our problem in a way it simply is not for an agency billing by the hour.

**3. Page one is a comparison.** The buyer we are built for is an owner whose competitors are consistently ranking for the phrases that matter. Winning that position does not put you alone on a page. It puts you side by side with those same competitors in one result list, being judged on the strength of what you look like. The brand is the thing being compared.

**4. So the sequence runs traffic, then high quality leads, then sales.** The brand is what decides whether the second beat survives the first.

None of that claims a brand-driven uplift of any kind, and nothing about the identity work is guaranteed. What is promised stays the one thing: page one of Google for the key phrases named in your contract, and in a month we are not delivering it, we keep working and your billing stops.

## S4 what-brand-work-covers

<!-- src: D-BRAND-RAILS-002, D-BRAND-RAILS-003, D-BRAND-RAILS-016 -->

Representative service copy pending confirmation. There is no published brand methodology to draw from, so this section is written to be replaced by the real one rather than defended.

**Identity marks.** A primary mark plus the variants a real business actually needs: horizontal, stacked, icon-only and single-colour. Most identity problems in practice are not the logo, they are the four versions of it nobody made.

**Palette.** A defined set with the contrast checked for accessibility, not a mood board. A palette that fails on a phone in daylight is a palette that will be quietly abandoned by whoever builds the next page.

**Type.** A working type system with a display role and a body role defined, chosen for how it behaves at the sizes your pages genuinely use rather than at headline size on a presentation slide.

**Tone.** How the business sounds in a heading, in a service page and in a follow-up email, written down so it can be applied by somebody who was not in the room when it was decided.

**Application.** The identity carried across the site templates, the campaign assets, the ads and the profiles, so the same business appears everywhere the campaign puts it. This is the part that is normally lost in the handover, and it is the reason the work sits inside one contract here.

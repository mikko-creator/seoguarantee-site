## S3 capability-deep-dive: the practice-area and jurisdiction phrase map [M]

Legal search is organised by matter type and bounded by jurisdiction, so a firm is never running one campaign.

The map has four columns: the practice area, the matter-type phrase itself, the jurisdiction or venue it is measured in, and the firms holding page one there today. The fourth column is what decides whether a phrase is worth contracting at all.

Two demand shapes sit inside it and they behave nothing alike, which is exactly why one strategy stretched across both is the failure this category gets attacked for.

**Crisis-driven matter search.** An accident, an arrest, an injury. The search happens within hours, on a phone, and the person contacts two or three firms off the first page and then stops looking. Time to contact is short and name recognition is close to irrelevant.

**Considered engagement search.** Business litigation, contracts, estate planning, employment. Researched across days or weeks, usually by somebody comparing firms deliberately, and carrying qualifiers that a person in crisis never types.

The jurisdiction rule, stated plainly: the set is proposed per practice area and per jurisdiction your firm actually takes matters in. Five practice areas across two states is not one phrase set, and this page is not going to pretend that a single list covers it.

Method of proposal: we read the firms currently on page one for each candidate phrase, propose a list from that read, and then the list is signed. Signing is what turns "delivered" into something checkable instead of something arguable.

## S5 capability-deep-dive: method, and the lines we will not cross [M]

Written on the assumption that you have already read the case against guaranteed search work, noticed that it quotes the search engines' own documentation, and arrived here to see whether the counter-argument survives contact.

Four charges, answered in order.

**Search engines warn against ranking guarantees.** They do, the warning describes a real pattern, and we are not going to argue with it. Nothing on this site suggests that any search engine approves of, permits or endorses an arrangement of this kind.

**You cannot guarantee what you do not control.** Correct. No one can guarantee a particular position, and this engagement does not purport to. What it guarantees is a term of business governing when a fee is owed.

**Guaranteed work may involve tactics that put a site at risk.** Answered by scope schedule rather than by assurance. Work performed: technical remediation of the site, a page for each contracted phrase written for the reader rather than the crawler, the local profile and its categories, internal linking, review flow, and the conversion work on the intake path. Work refused: purchased links, private blog networks, spun or padded text, phrase stacking, hidden text, and fabricated reviews. If any of those turns up in a proposal you are reading from anybody, you have your answer about the proposal.

**One-size-fits-all does not work.** Answered by the map above rather than by a paragraph here.

One limit, recorded because a legal reader will look for it: we do not review or clear your advertising against your state bar's rules. That responsibility stays inside your firm.

On the window, and it is not softened. The site currently publishes 6- and 12-month windows. Those are not yet an agreed contract term, and the engagement itself is assessed month by month. The published realism band for this work is three to six months, and the pattern buyers are warned about is page one in 30 days. A longer stated window is the honesty signal, not a weakness to hedge.

## S7 faq [S]

**Is a guaranteed ranking a red flag?**
As a general proposition, yes, and a supplier promising you a position by a date deserves suspicion. The distinction here is what the promise attaches to. We are not making a representation about how a search engine will behave. We are stating when a fee becomes payable.

**Who names the phrases?**
We propose them from a read of the current page one and they are named in the engagement you sign. You do not hold a veto over the list, and it is not open to substitution once signed. That is a real limit on your side and you should meet it here rather than at execution.

**What does a paused month actually mean for the work?**
The scope continues in full. Nobody comes off the account, the schedule does not change, and the billing stops until the positions are back on track. It happens automatically under the clause, so there is nothing for you to invoke and nothing to negotiate.

**How long?**
The current site publishes 6- and 12-month windows, which are not yet an agreed contract term. The engagement is checked against the named phrases every month throughout, never as a single test at the end.

**What happens on the call?**
A review of your market, the practice areas and jurisdictions in scope, and a proposed phrase list. It is not a branded product, it does not have a name, and nothing is signed on it.

The sequence runs a full year. Watch what happens to the invoice.

- **A month on target.** The agreed metrics are being delivered. The month is invoiced, at the retainer you agreed before you signed.

That is the whole mechanic. It is worth watching because it is the part of the offer that cannot be described convincingly in a sentence: the invoice is the thing that moves, and it moves on its own, because the contract says so.

**Heading.** How your key phrases get named.

The published charge against services like this one is that the agency guarantees phrases it can easily rank rather than phrases that produce work. It is a fair charge, and the answer to it is the naming process, not a reassurance.

**We run competitive research on your market first.** We look at who currently holds page one for the searches that produce work in your trade, in your geography, and at what those positions are worth to a business your size.

**We propose your key phrases.** Each one is proposed for a reason we will state out loud: commercial intent and buying moment. A phrase that is cheap to win and brings nobody who is ready to buy does not go on the schedule, because we would be the only party it benefits.

**They are named in the contract you sign.** The phrase schedule is attached to the agreement, with the geography set out beside each phrase. We propose, you sign. The counterweight that makes this credible: if we cannot commit to the phrases your business actually needs, we say so at the consultation. We would rather lose the work at the call than write a contract we cannot deliver against, because under this billing basis an undeliverable contract costs us the retainer anyway.

## S7 capability-showcase-3: what happens in a month we are off target

<!-- src: D-MARKET-001-RISK, D-OFFER-014 -->

**Heading.** A month we are off target.

Three things happen, and the third is the one the category does not offer.

**It is automatic, and it was written in before you signed.** The pause is a contract trigger, not a concession you have to ask for after a bad quarter. Nobody at this firm decides whether you have complained enough to deserve it. That is the entire distinction between this and the remedies buyers are warned about in this category, which are discretionary by design and can be declined.

**Nothing is handed back, because nothing was charged.** The obligation for that month is suspended, not erased after the fact. There is no reversal to chase and no credit note to negotiate, because the invoice for that month does not go out.

**The work does not stop. The billing does.** A month that is not invoiced is worked exactly like a month that is. Invoicing resumes on the condition the REMEDY ON MISS row in S2 states.

**The honest comparison.** The strongest agencies in this market offer month-to-month contracts and a commitment to consistent communication and complete transparency. That is real, and it is worth something: you are free to leave at any point. But freedom to leave is not a condition on the fee. The client still carries the whole outcome risk while the retainer is owed either way. That is a structural fact about how the category is sold, not a criticism of anyone selling it.

The mechanic above stands on the agreement, not on a story. It needs no precedent to be true, and we do not offer one. See S12 for why.

## S10 objection-answer

<!-- src: D-MARKET-002, D-INCUMBENT-001-OBJECTION -->

**Heading.** Is a guaranteed ranking a red flag? Seven answers, in the order the case against it is usually made.

If you have read the standard warning article about this category, you have read seven arguments. Here they are in order, answered on their merits.

**1. Search engines warn against guaranteed rankings. Conceded, and it goes first.**
Google Search Central states: "No one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google." Google's own beginner documentation goes further: "If they guarantee you that their changes will give you first place in search results, find someone else." Both are correct, and neither is a technicality we are about to work around. Nothing we do gives us control of a ranking algorithm, Google does not approve, endorse or permit a promise of position, and we make no suggestion otherwise. What is under our control is our own invoice. That is what the contract conditions.

**2. You cannot make a guarantee about something you cannot control. Correct, and we agree.**
Which is why what we guarantee is not the algorithm. It is the billing. We control when we bill, absolutely and unilaterally, and we have written into the contract that we do not bill in a month that fails the assessment in S2.

**3. A guaranteed service may involve tactics that risk a penalty.**
This is the fair version of the objection, because a fee tied to a position is a fee that rewards shortcuts. The answer is the published method in S5 and the standing commitment in S11: we do not use a tactic that puts your domain at risk of a search penalty. Note also what the billing basis does to the incentive. A penalty ends the positions, which ends the invoice, and the recovery work would then run at our cost for as long as it took.

**4. Guaranteed rankings may provide little benefit.**
The specific charge is that the guaranteed phrases are the easy ones. S5 is the answer: the phrases are proposed from competitive research, each for a reason we state out loud, and put on the schedule you sign, so you can read exactly what your money is tied to and judge whether those phrases would produce work for you.

**5. One-size-fits-all strategies do not work.**
Agreed, and the trade-specific pages are where that is answered rather than asserted. See the pages for dental practices, law firms, medical spas, HVAC businesses and contracting businesses. Each carries the substance of that trade, because a dental practice and an HVAC business are not competing for the same kind of search at the same moment.

**6. The wrong tactics can hurt your business.**
Also true, which is why S11 exists and why it states what this guarantee does not cover as plainly as the terms table states what it does.

**7. Page-one rankings in 30 days.**
This is the checkable one. The 6- and 12-month windows quoted and attributed in S6 sit inside the three-to-six-month band the category's own critics publish as realistic, and the agreement itself is assessed on the monthly cadence in S2. [OWNER PLACEHOLDER] The published windows are reported as what the current site says. They are not a ratified contract term and they are not asserted as one.

**One thing we will not claim.** This offer is a guaranteed SEO service by the standard definition: a service promising a specific result, typically first-page rankings for a set of key phrases, inside a timeframe. We are inside that category, not beside it, and saying otherwise would be falsifiable in a single search.

**The reframe this page was built to earn.** We are not asking you to believe the promise. We are asking you to check whether we delivered, before you pay for it.

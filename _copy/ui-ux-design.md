The objectives run in a fixed order: get traffic, generate high quality leads, convert those leads to sales. This page is about what happens between the first and the second.

**A position produces a visit, and nothing more.** That is the honest ceiling of a ranking. What the visit becomes is decided in the seconds after the click, on a page we either designed for that moment or did not.

**The person arriving typed something specific.** They had a particular problem, they described it in their own words, and a search engine sent them to you. If the page they land on makes them hunt for the thing they just typed, then the position we are contracted to hold has bought you a bounce.

**This is the buyer we are built for.** An owner whose competitors are consistently ranking for the phrases that matter to their business. Taking the position is the first half. Being the one they call is the second, and it is the half this page is responsible for.

The promise attached to all of it stays the same one: page one of Google for the phrases named in your contract, and in a month we are not delivering it, we keep working and your billing stops. The interface is not guaranteed, and no lift, rate or improvement is claimed anywhere on this page.

## S4 what-we-design

<!-- src: D-BRAND-RAILS-016, D-BRAND-RAILS-018 -->

Representative service copy, pending confirmation. Everything below except the navigation inventory is swappable, and it is written to be replaced rather than defended.

**Journeys.** The route from a search result to an enquiry, mapped per page template rather than assumed. A visitor landing on a vertical page has a different starting point from one landing on the home page, and pretending otherwise is how a journey gets designed for nobody.

**Navigation.** Menu structure and behaviour, including custom dynamic menus and multi-level menus, and the top and bottom panel toolbars that keep the primary action reachable wherever a visitor has scrolled to.

**Page templates.** What a service page, a vertical page, a location page and a contact page each have to do, and where the single primary action sits on each of them. One action per page, in a place the eye reaches without being told.

**Enquiry forms.** Field order, length and error behaviour, designed so that the form is the same action as the phone call rather than a competing one. A form that argues with the phone number next to it costs you both.

**Mobile behaviour.** Thumb reach, tap targets, sticky access to the call, and deciding what collapses rather than letting everything shrink.

**Client portal.** How you see interface work in progress and request more of it.

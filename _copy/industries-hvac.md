## S3 capability-deep-dive: emergency and seasonal phrase structure, and the local work behind it [M]

This section is method. It describes how the set is proposed and contracted, and it names no position we hold, because none is evidenced anywhere.

The trade splits its demand four ways and each way behaves differently.

**Emergency repair.** AC repair near me, emergency AC repair with a city, 24 hour HVAC with a city, furnace not working. Phone first, settled inside the hour, dominated by the map results, and almost entirely insensitive to price at the moment of search.

**Replacement and installation.** AC installation with a city, furnace replacement cost with a city, heat pump with a city. A different person on a different timeline: researched across days, compared across bids, and worth many times a repair call. Same company, completely different phrases, completely different competitors.

**Maintenance and plan searches.** The recurring-revenue tier. Small in volume and disproportionate in lifetime value, which is why it is worth contracting even though the numbers look unimpressive next to the emergency set.

**Commercial against residential.** Two separate demand pools that share a trade name and share almost no phrases. A contractor doing both is running two phrase sets, not one wider one.

Then the geography, which in this trade is the shape of the campaign rather than a footnote to it. The set is bounded by drive time, not by city limits. A metro with fifteen suburbs a truck can reach is a set of geo-modified variants, and a suburb the trucks do not serve stays out of the contract no matter how much volume it carries.

The local-listing work is part of the method, not an add-on. Emergency results are dominated by the map pack, so the profile, the categories, the service-area definition and the review flow all sit inside the scope of the work. That is a description of what gets done, not a claim about what it produces.

## S5 faq [S]

**Is a guaranteed ranking a red flag?**
The published warnings describe a real pattern and you should take them seriously. Nobody can promise a specific position on the page, and we do not. What is guaranteed here is a term of business about when an invoice is owed. No search engine approves of, permits or endorses this arrangement, and nothing on this site pretends one does.

**What will you do, and what will you not do?**
Done: technical repair of the site, a page for each contracted phrase, the local profile and its categories, service-area definition, internal linking, review flow, and the conversion work on the call path. Not done: purchased links, private blog networks, spun or padded text, phrase stacking, hidden text, and fabricated reviews. If a proposal you are reading from anyone contains one of those, that is your answer about the proposal.

**Who chooses the phrases?**
We propose them from a read of who currently answers those searches, and they are named in the contract you sign. There is no veto and nothing is substituted after the contract is signed.

**What does a paused month mean in the middle of peak season?**
This is the question that matters most in this trade, so here is the full answer. The work does not slow down. Nobody comes off the account, the schedule does not change, and the season is not treated as a reason to accept less. What stops is the billing, until results are back on track. The clause is in the contract before signature, so it operates on its own rather than being something you have to raise while you are busy.

**How long?**
The site currently publishes 6- and 12-month windows, which are not yet an agreed contract term, and the agreement is assessed month by month throughout. The industry's published realism band is three to six months and the pattern buyers are warned about is page one in 30 days, so the longer stated window is the honest one. Worth saying plainly: a campaign starting in spring and a campaign starting in autumn will look different at month three, and the monthly assessment is what keeps that from becoming an argument between us.

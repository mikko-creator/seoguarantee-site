## S4 is-your-conversion-engine-underperforming

<!-- src: D-OFFER-013, D-MARKET-002 -->

Four questions. They are diagnostic rather than rhetorical, and there is no threshold attached to any of them, because a benchmark quoted without its source is just a number with a confident voice.

- Do you know which pages your enquiries actually come from?
- Can you tell which of your key phrases produce calls rather than sessions?
- Do visitors who reach your service pages ever reach your contact page?
- Do you know what happens on mobile compared with desktop?

If the answer to most of those is no, the useful work is probably not more traffic yet.

There is a volume floor where this becomes measurable rather than anecdotal: around a thousand visitors a month or more. Below that, a change can look like an improvement for reasons that have nothing to do with the change.

And the honest half. Buyers are told to be sceptical of agencies that promise conversion outcomes, and that advice is sound. So we will say it plainly: we do not promise a conversion rate. We commit contractually to the ranking metrics, and we work continuously on conversion. The full argument for why that distinction is the honest one is on guarantee.

Six groups, and this is genuine first-party scope rather than a services list assembled for a web page.

**Analytics.** Heat-mapping, session recording and user testing. Watching what people actually do on the page rather than reasoning about what they ought to do.

**Market research.** What the buyer in your trade is comparing you against before they call you, which is usually two or three competitors you can name and one you cannot.

**Conversion strategy.** A home-page strategy and an individual-page strategy, kept separate on purpose, because the page that ranks for a service phrase has a different job from the front door and inherits none of its context.

**Creative service.** Senior writing, design and development applied directly to the pages that matter, rather than a recommendations document handed back for somebody else to build. This is the difference the one-fee model actually buys.

**A/B testing.** Described honestly: a month-after-month process rather than a single experiment with a finish line. Most tests teach you something and do not move anything, and the ones that move something are found by running the ones that do not.

**Personalization**, including the tools already used on live work, Optimizely and VWO.

All of it sits inside the Full Suite. We only charge for the SEO guarantee, and everything else is complementary.

## S6 how-a-conversion-change-is-measured

<!-- src: D-BRAND-RAILS-008, D-MARKET-001-PROOF -->

This is stated before any result is shown, because the order is the point. A figure produced by a method you were told about afterwards is a different thing from a figure produced by a method agreed in advance.

Every conversion change we report carries five things.

- **What was measured**, named explicitly. Enquiries, calls, bookings or sales, never the word performance.
- **The baseline it is measured against**, and the dates of that baseline.
- **The period the comparison covers.**
- **The tool the measurement was taken in.**
- **The statement that individual results vary**, and that we publish no rate for how often this work pays off.

From that follows the rule this page adopts publicly: we publish a conversion figure only with its baseline and its period attached, or we do not publish it.

That is a constraint, and it is deliberately also the argument. The published case against guarantees in this category is that they are vague and impossible to falsify. A number you can check the working on is the opposite of that, and no percentage, multiple or count appears anywhere on this page without its period, its baseline and its named source.
